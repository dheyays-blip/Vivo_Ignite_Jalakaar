About

What Jalaakar is.

The idea

India’s water data already exists. The Central Ground Water Board measures thousands of wells, BMC publishes lake levels every morning, IMD publishes rainfall. What none of it does is tell you what happens next — and by the time a borewell fails, the crop is already in the ground.

Jalaakar is a prediction layer over that public data. It forecasts where the water will be, turns the forecast into a 0–100 stress score, and sends a WhatsApp warning in Marathi, Hindi or English before the water runs out. No sensors, no hardware, no app to install.

What is actually running

  • 940 wells, 68,994 quality-controlled CGWB readings across 34 districts
  • 12 reservoirs, BMC and Pune Irrigation Department, current to yesterday
  • 3.8M rows of NASA POWER daily weather
  • A trained forecaster: 1.39 m error at 7 days, 24% better than a seasonal baseline
  • Scores for all 247 talukas — try one on the live demo

  • 2,584

Error is flat from 7 to 30 days because the last CGWB reading is 77 days old on average — staleness dominates, not forecast horizon. That is the measured case for community reporting: one fresh reading moves this number more than any model change.
The alert threshold is set to be right more often than it is wrong: at this cutoff about half of the alerts sent are real crises. Loosening it catches 77% of crises but makes three alerts in four false, which is how a warning channel gets ignored. The full curve is in reports/operating_point.json.
